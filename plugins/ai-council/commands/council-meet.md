description: Ask your council a question. It meets and brings back one clear answer here in the chat.
argument-hint: "<your question> [quick]"
---
Run a council meeting on the owner's question and bring the result back into this chat. This chat is the owner's main way to use the council; the hub (`/council-hub`) is only an optional overview. The owner's words: $ARGUMENTS

Talk to the owner in plain words, one short message at a time. No file paths, commands, session ids or technical terms in anything the owner reads.

1. **The question.** If the owner's words hold no question, ask what the council should decide, with the top three open matters from `outputs/council/AGENDA.md` as the answers (the owner can still type their own), and wait. Use a full meeting unless the owner says "quick"; mention once that "quick" gives a faster, lighter answer.
2. **Start it.** **Only for a big decision** (money, prices, offers, direction), first prepare **a short fact sheet for the question**, so members argue the decision rather than the facts: search `outputs/council/inputs/FACTS.md`, `BRIEF.md`, all the dossiers in `outputs/council/members/dossiers/` and the reading notes (use helpers if they are large); list **at most ten facts**, the ones that matter most for this decision, one line each with its status (checked, confirmed by the owner, or UNCONFIRMED), and flag any conflict between sources. Save it in `outputs/council/files/` as `fact-sheet-<short topic>.md`. For any other question there is no fact sheet: run the command below without it. Then, with the project's Python (`python3` on a Mac), run `tools/council/council.py --topic "<the question, in the owner's own words>" --files <the fact sheet>`, adding at most one dossier with another `--files` if the question needs its detail (copied into `outputs/council/files/` first), and `--quick` when asked, as a background job this chat is told about when it ends (the shell tool's background option), never detached with `&` or `nohup`, which gives no signal when the meeting stops. If the owner attached a file in this chat, save a copy into `outputs/council/files/` too and add it with `--files <name>`. When the question is about reviewing the business, a hard stretch, cash, habits or a plan, copy the skill's `references/business-strategies.md` (what 14 best-selling business authors share, with a review checklist and a hard-times plan) into `outputs/council/files/` once, and attach it, plus any case studies the council keeps, with another `--files` each: they are reference, never the owner's facts. **When the owner asks the council to read the business strategies or the case studies** (to review something or to advise), attach the file asked for the same way and end the topic with this line, naming that file: "(Every speaker: before your first view, open outputs/council/files/business-strategies.md with your Read tool, read all of it, then say which parts you used and which do not fit.)" Without that line members are shown only the start of a long file. Never start a meeting while another is running. Tell the owner in one line: "Your council is meeting now. It usually takes 3 to 10 minutes." Then wait quietly for the meeting to finish.
3. **If it did not run.** If the program's output says `NOT STARTED` (today's limit of meetings, another meeting still running, or the council paused), tell the owner that in one plain line and stop here: never read `--report latest` then, because it would show the previous meeting as if it were this one. If the program failed in any other way, run `tools/council/council.py --check-claude`. On SIGN-IN NEEDED, start the sign-in yourself as `/council-build` step 10 does (run the program it names with `auth login` in the background, tell the owner "A page will open in your browser. Click the button to approve.", wait for Done, check again), then start the meeting again. On USAGE, tell the owner in one line to try again later. Otherwise say in one plain line that the meeting could not run. Never show an error message as it came.
4. **Read the result** with `tools/council/council.py --report latest`. It prints the meeting's result: `conclusion`, `first_step`, `next_steps`, `needed`, `decided`, `questions` (each with `n`, `ask`, `options`, `if_skipped`, `state`) and `outcome`. **Then check it:** a helper on Sonnet compares the big figures and every rule or quote credited to a person in the minutes with `FACTS.md`, `BRIEF.md`, the fact sheet (when there is one) and the dossiers. Say nothing if all is right. If something is wrong or unconfirmed, add at most three plain lines under the answer in step 5: what it is and what the record says.
5. **Give the whole answer in the chat, told like a friend,** as the skill's `references/answer-style.md` says (read it first; revised 21 Nov 2026 when an owner asked for the full answer in the chat and more context on why each member said what). Real sentences, everyday words, plain headings, the council's meaning kept exactly and nothing added, in this order:
   - **In one line:** what this means for the owner.
   - **The overall picture:** where the owner stands and why the council sees it so, with the context from the owner's own words or record.
   - **What the council suggests:** the steps forward, numbered, each with one sentence on why (from `first_step`, `next_steps` and `needed`). Where the council weighed more than one road, name the roads and why it chose this one.
   - **What each member said, and why:** one short paragraph per member who spoke, from the minutes and the transcript: what they said, the principle or story behind it, and how it fits the owner's situation.
   - **Where they disagreed,** both sides and why, and where it landed.
   - **What changed since last time,** when there was a last time.
   - **What is still yours to decide:** at most three. Anything marked "proposed" is the council's suggestion, never the owner's decision.
   Leave out the costs unless asked. Then the visuals and, for a **big decision** (money, prices, offers, team, direction), the produced overview page (step 8), before any question.
6. **Ask the council's open questions, one at a time,** with the question tool: the question exactly as written, its `options` as the answers (the owner can still type their own; if it has fewer than two, offer Yes / No), and the `if_skipped` line as the help text. After each answer run `tools/council/council.py --answer latest <n> "<the owner's answer, word for word>"`. If the owner skips one, leave it open.
7. **Then one multiple-choice question:** "What next?" Meet again with my answers / Make an overview page / That is all for now.
   - **Meet again:** run `tools/council/council.py --follow-up <the session from the report> --quick` and repeat steps 4 to 7.
   - **Overview page:** see step 8.
8. **Visuals, and the produced overview page.** Build them only from this meeting's minutes, transcript and drafts (the minutes file named in the report, `outputs/council/sessions/<session>/` and `outputs/council/drafts/<session>/`). Never add a number, option or name the council did not write; draw a guess dashed or labelled "guess".
   - **In the chat**, with the app's built-in visual tool where this chat has one (following the dataviz skill): the decision (the roads weighed and the one chosen) and the steps forward on a simple timeline.
   - **The produced overview page** is made for every big decision and offered for the rest: one self-contained page, `outputs/council/visuals/<session>/overview.html`, opened for the owner, made for reading top to bottom as the skill's `references/answer-style.md` describes: the answer in one line; the overall picture; the steps forward on a timeline; a member map (one card per member: what they said, why, and where they stood, coloured by agree, agree with a change, or disagree); where they disagreed; the numbers as a simple chart with guesses dashed; what is still the owner's to decide. Plain and high contrast: black or white text on solid colour, large type, one strong colour for the main point, no decoration. Slides only if asked.
9. **"Why?"** If the owner asks why the council decided something, read the minutes and the meeting record and answer in a few plain sentences.

If no council exists yet, say so in one line and run `/council-build` instead. Nothing is published, sent or bought.
