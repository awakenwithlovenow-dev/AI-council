description: Ask your council a question. It meets and brings back one clear answer here in the chat.
argument-hint: "<your question> [quick]"
---
Run a council meeting on the owner's question and bring the result back into this chat. This chat is the owner's main way to use the council; the hub (`/council-hub`) is only an optional overview. The owner's words: $ARGUMENTS

Talk to the owner in plain words, one short message at a time. No file paths, commands, session ids or technical terms in anything the owner reads.

1. **The question.** If the owner's words hold no question, ask what the council should decide, with the top three open matters from `outputs/council/AGENDA.md` as the answers (the owner can still type their own), and wait. Use a full meeting unless the owner says "quick"; mention once that "quick" gives a faster, lighter answer.
2. **Start it.** **Only for a big decision** (money, prices, offers, direction), first prepare **a short fact sheet for the question**, so members argue the decision rather than the facts: search `outputs/council/inputs/FACTS.md`, `BRIEF.md`, all the dossiers in `outputs/council/members/dossiers/` and the reading notes (use helpers if they are large); list **at most ten facts**, the ones that matter most for this decision, one line each with its status (checked, confirmed by the owner, or UNCONFIRMED), and flag any conflict between sources. Save it in `outputs/council/files/` as `fact-sheet-<short topic>.md`. For any other question there is no fact sheet: run the command below without it. Then, with the project's Python (`python3` on a Mac), run `tools/council/council.py --topic "<the question, in the owner's own words>" --files <the fact sheet>`, adding at most one dossier with another `--files` if the question needs its detail (copied into `outputs/council/files/` first), and `--quick` when asked, as a background job this chat is told about when it ends (the shell tool's background option), never detached with `&` or `nohup`, which gives no signal when the meeting stops. If the owner attached a file in this chat, save a copy into `outputs/council/files/` too and add it with `--files <name>`. Never start a meeting while another is running. Tell the owner in one line: "Your council is meeting now. It usually takes 3 to 10 minutes." Then wait quietly for the meeting to finish.
3. **If it did not run.** If the program's output says `NOT STARTED` (today's limit of meetings, another meeting still running, or the council paused), tell the owner that in one plain line and stop here: never read `--report latest` then, because it would show the previous meeting as if it were this one. If the program failed in any other way, run `tools/council/council.py --check-claude`. On SIGN-IN NEEDED, start the sign-in yourself as `/council-build` step 10 does (run the program it names with `auth login` in the background, tell the owner "A page will open in your browser. Click the button to approve.", wait for Done, check again), then start the meeting again. On USAGE, tell the owner in one line to try again later. Otherwise say in one plain line that the meeting could not run. Never show an error message as it came.
4. **Read the result** with `tools/council/council.py --report latest`. It prints the meeting's result: `conclusion`, `first_step`, `next_steps`, `needed`, `decided`, `questions` (each with `n`, `ask`, `options`, `if_skipped`, `state`) and `outcome`. **Then check it:** a helper on Sonnet compares the big figures and every rule or quote credited to a person in the minutes with `FACTS.md`, `BRIEF.md`, the fact sheet (when there is one) and the dossiers. Say nothing if all is right. If something is wrong or unconfirmed, add at most three plain lines under the answer in step 5: what it is and what the record says.
5. **Show the answer** as the skill's `references/answer-style.md` says (read it first; 21 Nov 2026: an owner found word-for-word minutes and stacks of bold labels "erratic, chaotic and unorganised"). About one screen, in a warm friend's everyday words, with the council's meaning kept exactly and nothing added:
   - **In one line:** what this means for the owner (from `conclusion`).
   - **What the council thinks:** two or three short paragraphs.
   - **What changed since last time**, when this follows an earlier meeting or answer.
   - **What you do next:** numbered, at most five, from `first_step` and `next_steps`, with what is needed folded in.
   - **What is still yours to decide:** at most three. Anything in `decided` marked "proposed" is the council's suggestion and is never written as the owner's decision.
   Leave out the debate, the outcome record and the costs unless the owner asks. For a **major decision** (money, prices, team, direction), add where each member stood, one sentence each, and offer the overview page for the rest (the options weighed, the facts relied on with their status, the risks, what would change it).
   Then show **the two visuals** (step 8), before any question.
6. **Ask the council's open questions, one at a time,** with the question tool: the question exactly as written, its `options` as the answers (the owner can still type their own; if it has fewer than two, offer Yes / No), and the `if_skipped` line as the help text. After each answer run `tools/council/council.py --answer latest <n> "<the owner's answer, word for word>"`. If the owner skips one, leave it open.
7. **Then one multiple-choice question:** "What next?" Meet again with my answers / Make an overview page / That is all for now.
   - **Meet again:** run `tools/council/council.py --follow-up <the session from the report> --quick` and repeat steps 4 to 7.
   - **Overview page:** see step 8.
8. **Two visuals every meeting, an overview page on request.** Build them only from this meeting's own minutes and drafts (the minutes file named in the report, and `outputs/council/drafts/<session>/`). Never add a number, option or name the council did not write; mark assumed figures as assumed; keep the words plain and short.
   - **Simple, clear, direct, strong contrast:** each visual says one thing, and its title says it as a plain sentence. At most five items. Large text. Solid colour blocks with white text, or black text on white; no pale tints, no small grey print. One strong colour for the answer, grey for the rest. No decoration, and no legend or footnote unless the visual cannot be read without it.
   - **Visual 1, the decision:** the options the council weighed and the one it chose, where each seated member stood (agreed, objected, disagreed), and how strong the evidence was (from the minutes' outcome record).
   - **Visual 2, what happens next:** the first step and the steps after it, with the dates the minutes set for checking the result. If the meeting wrote figures (prices, targets, counts), add a simple chart of them here.
   - **Guesses look like guesses:** an assumed figure is drawn dashed or labelled "guess", never like a fact.
   Draw them with the app's built-in visual tool when this chat has one (it draws proper charts and diagrams in the chat), following the dataviz skill when it is available; otherwise write one simple self-contained HTML page holding both into `outputs/council/visuals/<session>/` and open it. On "Make an overview page" (the one home for the detail; the chat never repeats it): one simple self-contained page, `outputs/council/visuals/<session>/overview.html`, in the same plain, high-contrast style, holding the conclusion and first step, next steps and what is needed, the options weighed and why this one won, where each member stood and why, the facts relied on with their status, the risks and what would change the decision, the open questions, and both visuals; open it for the owner. Slides only if the owner asks for them: one idea per slide, in the same order.
9. **"Why?"** If the owner asks why the council decided something, read the minutes and the meeting record and answer in a few plain sentences.

If no council exists yet, say so in one line and run `/council-build` instead. Nothing is published, sent or bought.
