Build an AI council for this project from the owner's own material. Load the **ai-council** skill first and follow it; also load **headless-ai-calls** and **local-app-build** before changing any code.

Sources named by the owner: $ARGUMENTS

## How to talk to the owner (this overrides the skill's intake wording)

The owner may never have used Claude on a computer. Guide them like a first-time user.
- **One step per message.** Say what to click or type, in one or two short sentences, then stop and wait.
- **Explain only what they must do.** Do not describe what you are doing behind the scenes, the folders, the files or the method. A short progress line now and then is enough ("Reading your email: 400 of 1,200").
- **Every question is multiple choice.** Use the question tool with two to four ready answers. Never ask the owner to type an answer, a name, a path or a setting. If you need a fact, find it in their material and offer it as a choice to confirm.
- **Do the technical parts yourself.** Never ask the owner to run a command you can run.
- Plain words. No jargon ("connector" becomes "connect your email").

## The steps

**If a council already exists here** (`outputs/council/members/approved/` holds members), this is a refresh, not a new build. First bring the council program up to date: run the skill's `scripts/new_council.py --project . --update-program` with the same Python, and say in one line whether anything was updated (the council's own files are never touched; old program files are kept). Skip steps 1, 3, 5 to 8 and 10 unless the owner asks for them. In step 2 ask only about apps not yet connected. In step 4 read only items newer than the dates in `outputs/council/inputs/COVERAGE.md`, at the same three levels, and update `BRIEF.md`, the dossiers and `COVERAGE.md`, marking each change with its date. In step 9 add new matters to `AGENDA.md` without removing the owner's. Then offer `/council-review`.

1. **Set up, silently.** Find Python 3 (`python3` on a Mac). If it is missing, guide the install one click at a time. Run the skill's `scripts/new_council.py --project .` with it (the skill's base directory is shown when it loads). Say only: "Your council's home is ready." Then tell the owner once, in one line: "While I work, Claude may ask you to allow a step, and your Mac may ask once to let Claude see a folder such as Downloads. Both are normal: click Allow."
2. **Connect, one app at a time.** Check which apps this session can already read. Then, for each of these in turn, ask one multiple-choice question ("Do you use Gmail for work?" Yes / No / A different one): work email, documents (Google Drive, Notion or folders on this computer), meeting and call recordings or transcripts (Zoom, Granola, Fireflies, Otter), messages (Slack or similar), and the website or sales pages (find the address in their material and offer it to confirm).
   - For each app they use that is not connected, give the steps exactly as this session shows them, one per message, and after each ask "Did that work?" (Yes / No / Skip this one). On No, try once more with a simpler route, then skip it and note it.
   - Test each connection by reading one item, and say "Connected: your email" in one line.
   - Several accounts of the same app (for example two or three Gmail boxes): export each once (Google Takeout, Mail only, MBOX) into `outputs/council/inputs/raw/<app>-<account>/` and read the files, instead of switching accounts back and forth. Until the exports arrive, finish one account completely before asking to switch.
   - An app with no connector: use its own export, or read it in a browser tab the owner signs in to, read only, asking before each download. Look first for the same material where it is already readable (drafts often sit in documents or chat).
   - Read only. Never write to, send from or delete in a source.
3. **One question on limits, multiple choice, several answers allowed:** "Is anything off limits?" Read all my work material / Leave out personal and family email / Leave out money and banking / Leave out staff and HR.
4. **Read all of it, in stages, quietly.** The owner should not have to explain their business; the material does that.
   - **Inventory first:** list what exists in each source and save it as `outputs/council/inputs/INVENTORY.md` with counts. Tell the owner the totals in one line and that the reading may take more than one sitting.
   - **Sort before reading, into three levels,** and record the count of each in the coverage record. The owner's limits from step 3 come first; when unsure, choose the higher level.
     - **Read in full:** the owner's own sent messages; conversations with customers or people who might buy; anything about offers, prices, sales, refunds or complaints; call notes and transcripts; documents the owner wrote; the website and sales pages.
     - **Skim:** everyday team chat, older threads, documents by others. Take out only decisions, numbers, problems and short quotes, each with its date.
     - **Count, do not read:** newsletters, receipts, automatic notifications, other people's marketing.
   - **Helpers read, you check.** Give batches (about 50 items, or one Slack channel-week) to helper subagents on a cheaper model (Sonnet for full reading, Haiku for skimming), up to ten at a time: more at once finishes sooner and uses no more of the owner's allowance in total. If a helper reports a usage limit, start no new helpers, let the running ones finish, tell the owner in one line when to come back, and continue from the coverage record; a batch cut off midway is read again. Each helper gets the skill's `references/reader-brief.md` (the same instructions for every helper), the owner's limits from step 3, its level and its batch. It reads the batch read-only (fetching it through the connected app, or from a copy you saved in `outputs/council/inputs/raw/` when it cannot reach the app) and writes notes to `outputs/council/inputs/notes/<source>-<batch>.md`: facts with date and source, decisions, numbers, problems, short quotes in the customer's or owner's own words, and open questions. Helpers never write the brief or the dossiers. Newest first. Save a text copy of each source that matters into `outputs/council/inputs/`.
   - **Check each batch as it lands, not afterwards.** Read every note. Check one item in ten against its original. Check every decision-critical fact 100% against its source, with a fresh checker on the strongest model at high effort: every price and payment plan, every money figure (with its currency, and actual or forecast), every quote in the owner's name, and every rule a member may veto on. Correct or drop what does not match, and say so in the note.
   - **Keep one facts register,** `outputs/council/inputs/FACTS.md`, a table: ID | fact | value | source date | exact source | kind (decided / proposed / said by <name> / heard on a call / inferred) | checked (yes / partly / no / not found) | confirmed by the owner. A decision-critical fact that is partly checked, not found, only heard on a call, written by someone else, or contradicted by another source is marked **UNCONFIRMED**.
   - **Keep a coverage record,** `outputs/council/inputs/COVERAGE.md`: every source, how many items exist, how many fell in each level, how many were read or skimmed, what was skipped and why, and what was checked. Never say everything was read unless this record shows it. After the inventory, tell the owner in one line roughly how long the reading will take.
   - **If the allowance runs out,** stop cleanly, tell the owner in one line when to come back, and continue from the record.
   - **Build the owner's profile, `outputs/council/BRIEF.md`, from the facts register,** with register IDs beside the figures: who they are, what the business sells and to whom, offers and prices, how customers arrive and why they stay or leave, the owner's own way of speaking (quoted from their writing), the numbers that exist, and the decisions that keep coming back. Every statement carries its source and date; keep **decided** apart from **discussed**; mark anything inferred. Also save the owner's way of speaking, with the quotes, as `outputs/council/inputs/VOICE-PROFILE.md`: drafts written in the owner's voice read it first.
   - **Count the recurring decisions.** In `COVERAGE.md`, list each decision or problem that keeps coming back, with how often it appears and where ("pricing: 41 of 380 emails, 6 of 12 call notes").
   - **Write one dossier per area of the business** those decisions fall in (for example offers and prices, finding clients, the client's experience, money and time, the owner's voice) in `outputs/council/members/dossiers/`, citing source and date.
5. **Choose the seats, then write the members.** Follow "How the seats are chosen" in `references/member-design.md`. In short:
   - **Decisions first.** A seat exists to argue the decisions that recur in the material. One seat per area that carries real weight, three to seven in all, the fewest that cover everything. Fold a small or near-duplicate area into its neighbour; an area with little material becomes a **provisional** seat (it may object, never veto) or is folded in.
   - **Plus one creative seat** that proposes the boldest true version first. Without it the council only finds faults.
   - **Different angles, never forced disagreement.** Each seat gets a conviction from the material, labelled as a hypothesis. On a real decision from the material, two seats that would say the same thing are one seat: merge them. Never make a seat contrary for its own sake.
   - **Red lines come from the owner's own rules,** found in the material or given in step 3. A preference is never a veto.
   - Members are **roles, never imitations of real people** named in the material (staff, clients, mentors). Write each with `references/member-design.md`: what the seat knows how to do, its conviction, the questions it always asks, its red lines, its voice and its boundaries with neighbouring seats. **Prices, offers, plans, numbers and past events never go in a member file** (two or three short, dated calibration lessons excepted): they date, and a council once argued from stale prices that sat in its member files. Put them in `BRIEF.md` with their date and source. Keep each member file to about one page (900 to 1,100 words): a test found longer files no more useful and more expensive. End every member file with this line: "Before you state a figure or credit a rule or quote to anyone, check it in BRIEF.md, FACTS.md or the dossier, and call anything unconfirmed 'unconfirmed'."
   - Write the members to `outputs/council/members/final/`, and fill the seat table in `outputs/council/members/final/SEATS.md` (from the template already there): one row per seat with its member, status, remit and what it was built from, including the reason in the owner's material with its count ("pricing comes up in 41 of 380 emails"). The co-chair chooses each meeting's seats from this table.
6. **Independent review, at most two rounds.** Give `prompts/03-CROSS-REVIEW.md`, the member files, the seat table, the dossiers and the brief, and nothing of your own reasoning, to a subagent on a different model (Sonnet if you run on Opus, Opus if you run on Sonnet). Apply its corrections, keeping them visible. Anything still disputed after the second round is cut, or kept only as a labelled hypothesis.
7. **Confirm, by multiple choice only.** First, **the facts page:** one simple page, like the overview page `/council-review` makes, with the 25 most important facts that are UNCONFIRMED or that the council will lean on most. Each is a plain sentence with its source, marked True / Not quite / Not true, with room for the owner's own words. Open it, and when the owner pastes the answers back, file them in `outputs/council/memory/RULINGS.md` and the register ("confirmed by the owner", with the date), and correct every file that used them, keeping the old line struck through. The rest stay unconfirmed until a later round or a meeting asks. Then ask only what truly needs the owner's word, one question at a time, each with ready answers drawn from their own material and your recommendation first:
   - their name as the hub should greet them (the forms found in their material);
   - what the business is, in one sentence (two or three versions to choose from);
   - what the council is for (the two or three recurring decisions you found);
   - what it must never do (several answers allowed: spend money, publish, contact people, change prices);
   - each proposed member in one line, under a plain working name for its role ("Sales Lead"), with the reason from their own material in a few words ("pricing comes up in 41 of 380 emails"): Keep / Leave out. A left-out seat's remit passes to its nearest neighbour; note that in the seat table;
   - anything in the profile you could not settle from the material, as "Which is right?" with the candidates.
   Record every answer in `BRIEF.md`, the charter `outputs/council/CHARTER.md` (replace `<OWNER>` with their name, fill rules 4 and 10, and delete the template note at its top), `outputs/council/memory/RULINGS.md` and `config.json` (`owner_name`). Do not ask about anything the material already settles.
8. **Role models (optional).** A role model is a seat built only from a public figure's own recorded words, read word for word: a lens on their approach, never an imitation of the person, always named in full.
   - **Packs first.** Look for role-model packs the owner was given: a zip whose name ends in `-role-model.zip` in their Downloads, on the Desktop or in this folder, and folders already in `outputs/council/members/role-models/`, and loose role-model files the owner put in this folder (a `member-<name>.md` with its `dossier.md`). Unpack or move each into `outputs/council/members/role-models/<name>/`, never straight into `members/approved/`. A pack without a `roster-line.txt` gets its seat-table row written from the member file's **Seat** line.
   - For each pack, one question: "Add <full name> to your council as a role model? This seat speaks from their own recorded words." (Keep / Leave out). On Keep, copy its `member-<name>.md` into `members/final/` and add the row in its `roster-line.txt` to the seat table in `members/final/SEATS.md` (the co-chair chooses seats from that table, so a role model missing from it is left out of meetings in its own field). Later, once the council is running, the same steps add a role model whenever the owner asks, with the row going into `members/approved/SEATS-ROSTER.txt` and the file into `members/approved/`.
   - **Then:** "Is there anyone else whose work you look up to?" with up to three names the owner's own material quotes, follows or mentions, plus "No one else" (several answers allowed). For each name chosen, say in one line that this takes time and usage and offer "Now / Later". To build one, load the **persona-research** skill and follow it exactly: the four-point bar first (anyone who falls short becomes a case study, not a seat), one person at a time, every line sourced. Write the research, a `dossier.md`, a `member-<name>.md` and a one-row `roster-line.txt` (role, full name marked provisional, field) into `outputs/council/members/role-models/<name>/`, in the same layout as a pack, then ask Keep / Leave out as above.
   - A role-model member file says `Status: provisional` (it may object, never veto), points to its own dossier, lists where the person is silent, never puts an unrecorded opinion in their mouth, and ends with the same check line as every other member.
9. **Bring the important matters to the table.** From everything read, write the council's first agenda in `outputs/council/AGENDA.md`: the five to seven decisions and matters that most need tackling, most important first. For each: one plain sentence, why it matters now, what the material shows (with source and date), and what a good result would produce. Choose by what recurs, what is overdue, what money or customers depend on, and what the owner keeps putting off. Show the owner the list as one multiple-choice question, several answers allowed ("Which of these should your council take on?"), with your top three ticked first, and keep the rest lower on the agenda.
10. **Check the council can work on its own.** Run `tools/council/council.py --check-claude` with the same Python. It makes one small real call the way the desktop icon will. If it prints READY, say nothing. If it prints SIGN-IN NEEDED, start the sign-in yourself: run the program it names with `auth login` in the background, tell the owner in one line ("A page will open in your browser. Click the button to approve."), wait for "Done" (Done / It did not open), then run the check again. If it still fails after two tries, say so in one plain line and go on: the hub will open, but meetings will not run until this check passes.
11. **Finish.** Copy the kept members, role models included, from `members/final/` to `members/approved/`. Save the seat table, with only the kept seats, as `members/approved/SEATS-ROSTER.txt` (plain text, so it is never read as a member). Run one dry run. Last question: "Shall your council take a first look at where you stand now?" (Yes / Later). On Yes, run it exactly as `/council-review` does, here in this chat: the owner sees the whole picture before any single decision. Then offer the council's first matter: "Shall your council meet on <the top matter the owner ticked in step 9> now?" (Yes / Later). On Yes, run it exactly as `/council-meet` does. Then tell the owner, in three lines at most: to ask the council about one decision, come back to this chat in the sidebar and type `/council-meet` and the question (`/council-meet` alone offers the next matters on the council's list); `/council-review` looks at the whole picture again; `/council-hub` opens an overview window, if they ever want one.

**Decided afterwards, not during this build:** the look of the hub (colours, emblem), the council's own name, and the members' final names and signs. Use the standard look and plain working role names for now, and do not ask about any of them. They are settled later, once the owner has seen the council work.

Nothing leaves the project folder. Nothing is published, sent or bought.
