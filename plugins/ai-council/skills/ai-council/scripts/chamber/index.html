How the council works

You give the council a topic, any files it should read, and choose how deep it goes and whether you attend. You can follow it on the Meeting page; the Start window says roughly how long it will take.

  1. Opening. A co-chair names the topic and picks the member best suited to lead it.
  2. Own views. Every member writes their own view at the same moment, without seeing anyone else's, so nobody simply follows whoever spoke first.
  3. Big idea. The lead pitches the most ambitious version of what you could do.
  4. Discussion. The members respond, challenge each other and hand the floor on. Anyone who moves away from their own view says what changed their mind.
  5. Decision. The lead writes up what was agreed, how strong the evidence is, what would overturn it, and any drafts you would use.
  6. Sign-off. Claude and the Challenger, the two co-chairs, both have to accept it. Each first checks for an objection that was dropped, a claim with no source, or an assumption nobody tested. If one finds a real problem, the lead gets one round to fix it. If a draft changes after one of them accepted it, both sign off again on the final version.

A quick view skips the big idea and the discussion: own views, then the decision and the sign-off. It uses about half as much.

After a meeting the minutes say how strong the evidence is, who disagreed and why, and what the meeting changed compared with Claude's own first view. Decisions that real results can test go into the track record in the Library with a date. When it comes, mark whether it worked; the council reads its track record before every meeting.

Speaking to the council. Type or speak in the box at the bottom of the Meeting page. During a meeting they hear it before the next turn; start with a name, like "Research Lead, ...", and that member answers next. Open any turn and use "Reply to this" to comment on it. After a meeting, your note can start a follow-up meeting.

Talk with Claude is a one-to-one conversation before anything goes to the council. When it is ready, "Take this to the council" saves the conversation as a file and starts a meeting with it.

The microphone turns speech into text in Edge or Chrome, which send the audio to their own speech service to do it. In any browser you can also click in a box and press Windows + H.

Each meeting and each message to Claude uses your Claude allowance. If it runs out mid-meeting, the meeting waits and carries on by itself when it returns. Nothing is ever published or sent.
