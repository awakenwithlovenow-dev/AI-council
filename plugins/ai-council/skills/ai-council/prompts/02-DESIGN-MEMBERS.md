# Prompt 02: Design the members from the dossiers

Give this to the lead AI (Claude, or the strongest model available) together with **every** DOSSIER and KEEP file, the BRIEF and the RULINGS. Step 01 (`references/extraction-prompt.md`) produces the dossiers.

---

You are designing the persona members of my AI council from the dossiers attached. Each dossier came from one of my projects. Your output will be reviewed line by line by a second AI, so every claim must cite its source line.

**Before designing, list what you received:** each file, its project, its date range, its stated coverage and confidence. Note any file referenced but not supplied. If two reviewers may have received different file sets, say so: different inputs change conclusions.

**Then produce six things, in this order.**

### 1. The seats
Propose the **fewest distinct seats** that cover the material. One seat per real body of work. Merge a project into another seat when its history ends where the other begins, or when a separate seat would push the council toward something the owner has ruled out. Prefer strengthening an existing seat over adding a near-duplicate.

Include **one creative seat** whose job is to propose the boldest true version first. Without it, a council of checkers finds faults but never imagines. A seat built without its own project dossier is **provisional**: it may APPROVE or OBJECT, never VETO, until real evidence exists. For each seat give a title, its remit in one line, its boundaries with each neighbouring seat, and which dossier it comes from.

### 2. One profile per seat (900-1,100 words each)
Use this structure:
- **Status** (full or provisional).
- **Seat and boundaries.**
- **Conviction:** one or two sentences, labelled as a hypothesis, with its evidence. It must not smuggle in an untested benchmark.
- **Evidence discipline:** the provenance tags `[USER]`, `[USER (mem)]`, `[USER (sum)]`, `[FILE]`, `[AI-DRAFT]` and `[UNCERTAIN]` never upgrade by repetition; a "canon" or "locked" label is not infallible; other people's numbers are never the owner's benchmarks.
- **Best qualities,** each with a source line.
- **What is cut from the source:** its blind spots, which the member must not carry.
- **5 to 7 questions it always asks.**
- **VETO:** concrete breaches of recorded rules only. Everything else gets an OBJECT with the smallest repair; a creative seat adds a stronger true alternative.
- **Reference facts it carries:** the verified and excluded names, the standing rulings, and the numbers with their status. A member without its reference list will invent a gap.
- **Voice:** two or three sentences.
- **2-3 calibration examples from real events:** the situation, what it would have said, what happened. Never constructed examples, unless the seat is marked provisional.
- **Source key.**

### 3. Conflict register (for the owner)
Every place two dossiers, or a dossier and the owner's rulings, disagree. Give both versions with lines and dates, and say which is newer. Before calling something a conflict, **check the time scope** ("no interest *before* X" does not contradict a later experience) and **the domain** (years of practice, versus years with a subject, versus years before an event). Do not resolve conflicts yourself.

### 4. Precedent register
What was actually decided, tried and learned across all the dossiers:
- prices or terms actually set;
- attempts and their outcomes, with numbers and their status;
- standing rulings;
- corrections that must not be undone;
- open questions.

Tag each item.

### 5. Testimonial and proof status (if relevant)
For each quote, keep four separate statuses: supplied text, original speaker, verbatim accuracy, permission. Matching a quote to another source is a lead, not verification. Anonymising a quote verifies nothing.

### 6. What you are unsure of
The three to five places where your design most depends on something you could not verify.

**Rules throughout:**
- Invent nothing.
- Read the neighbouring lines before quoting any line.
- Scope every claim to what the source shows. Words like "first-hand", "the only", "no data exists" and "the biggest" are usually overstatements.
- Check claims in the owner's favour as hard as claims against.
- Preserve the owner's own wording.
- No member speaks for, or impersonates, a real person.
