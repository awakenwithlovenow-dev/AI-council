# Author strategy research
What worked on a real research of 14 business authors in five passes (November 2026), and what it cost to learn.

## Choosing the authors
- Pick for the question (reviewing a business, cash, team, habits, hard times), one or two per angle, and write down who was left out and why.
- Leave out people the council already seats or another thread is already researching.

## Where to read (never a whole book)
- In order of trust: the author's own free pages, articles, PDFs and free chapters; the author's own words in interviews and talks (a transcript panel or a published transcript); publisher or retailer samples; second-hand summaries only to fill a gap, and labelled.
- **Many authors put audiobooks and long talks on their own podcast feed.** Download audio only from that host, each file in its own folder outside the project, and transcribe it on the owner's computer with the local tool (see persona-research). This replaced most email forms.
- Mark every source with how it was read (AUTHOR-FREE, INTERVIEW, EXCERPT, SECOND-HAND, LEAD ONLY, or [K] from memory) and give it a reading code.
- **Email-gated free chapters are the owner's to submit.** Never type the owner's email without an explicit yes in chat; untick marketing boxes; say first that the form may still sign them up to a newsletter (one did). A drop folder with instructions lets the owner fetch them.
- **Never save book text.** Notes in your own words only; a browsing agent that tried to save a book sample stopped on an output filter.

## Checks
- **Quotes by script** against the saved page text or transcript, comparing letters and digits only (204 quotes: 193 found by search, 7 by hand, 4 left out). Short quotes only.
- **A rule credited to an author is checked against the author's own text or audio, conditions included.** A second-hand reading had Hormozi's 30-day cash rule wrong until his audiobook was heard.
- Settle apparent contradictions on the page (two tellings of one event, a garbled PDF text layer) before calling them disagreements.
- Browsing agents run one at a time across all threads; reading agents using plain fetches can run up to three at once, one fetch at a time each.

## The strategies file (what a council can use)
0. How far to trust this: what was and was not read.
1. The strategies most authors share, ranked by how many hold them.
2. Where they disagree.
3. A one-page business-review checklist.
4. A one-page hard-times plan: survive first, then thrive.
5. Open threads, each with what would test it.
6. One short section per author, pointing at the author files.

Beside it: `SOURCES.md` (every item, how it was read, its code), `WORKLOG.md` written as each pass lands, and `authors/<name>.md` with `-pass2`, `-pass3` files added rather than rewriting earlier passes.

## Private and public versions
- The full file, extracts, transcripts and downloaded chapters stay private.
- A public version (for a template or another owner) holds sections 1 to 4 in your own words, with reading codes, quotes and every personal reference removed. After stripping codes by regex, search for debris (doubled commas, empty brackets, orphan codes) and read it end to end before publishing.
- In a council it is reference: attached when a question needs it, never a member's voice and never the owner's facts (see the ai-council skill).
